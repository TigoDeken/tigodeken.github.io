A replacement intake for older carburetted bikes that bolts onto the existing carburettor and boot. Concept stage: no test results yet.

Manufacturing skill: fit-up tolerances, prototype testing

Problem and constraints

Older carburetted motorcycles often run with a cracked or missing original airbox, or with a universal pod filter. A pod filter fits easily but changes how air reaches the carburettor, which affects the mixture. I want an intake that fits a specific bike and keeps the carburettor working as intended.

  • Fit the existing carburettor mouth and intake boot without modifying the bike.
  • Survive engine heat, vibration, fuel vapour and rain.
  • Keep the filter serviceable with basic tools.
  • Be reversible, so the bike can go back to standard.

Method

The intake breaks down into three parts, each with its own design question:

  • Carburettor interface. Measure the carburettor mouth and boot, then design a clamped sleeve with a fit that seals without forcing the rubber.
  • Velocity stack. A bellmouth entry to guide air into the carburettor. Its profile and length are the main variables.
  • Filter housing. Sized around an off-the-shelf filter element, with a drain path for water and a cover that comes off without removing the intake.

Material for the final part depends on the temperatures measured near the cylinder. A printed part is only a fit and flow prototype.
